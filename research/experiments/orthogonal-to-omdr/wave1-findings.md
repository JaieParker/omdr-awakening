## Iteration 1: Selection Bias — "You Can't Stand Outside the Filter"
**Observer:** Sonnet (no OMDR context)

The selection bias critique has a fatal circularity: if observers ARE themselves standing waves that emerged from noise, then "what reality is" vs "what persists long enough to be observed" collapses. There is no view from outside the filter. The observer and the observed are both outputs of the same selection.

Also: the critique proves too much. If consonance is "just what survives," then so is the inverse square law, the periodic table, pi. Either all of physics is a selection artifact (and we have nothing), or "survives observation" is doing real epistemic work.

**Key vulnerability exposed:** OMDR may conflate "consonance is ontologically fundamental" with "consonance is the stable attractor of any system with reflection." The second is defensible without the first.

**Testable question:** Is there a domain where simple consonance FAILS to survive despite being simple? That would distinguish "fundamental" from "just robust."

## Iteration 2: Information Gradient — "Expressiveness Is Prediction Error"
**Observer:** Sonnet (no OMDR context)

The palette metaphor is wrong. Expressiveness isn't IN the ratio — it's in the LISTENER'S prediction error against internalized consonance. 17:13 is only expressive if the listener has already absorbed 3:2 as scaffolding. Strip the scaffolding and the complex ratio is just noise.

The hierarchy isn't a ranking OR a palette. It's a **dependency structure**. Complex ratios parasitize simple ones. You can't use dissonance expressively until consonance is load-bearing in the listener's nervous system.

**Time dimension:** Simple ratios persist in working memory (computationally cheap to maintain). Complex ratios fade. The "palette" metaphor is a photograph of a river — it kills the temporal dimension.

**Testable question:** What is the MINIMUM consonance load required for a given dissonance to register as expressive rather than random? This applies beyond music — to ecosystems, cognition, relationships.

## Iteration 3: Pairwise Limitation — "The Criticism Is Half-Wrong"
**Observer:** Sonnet (no OMDR context)

The Stern-Brocot tree is already a 3-body operation (mediant of two parents → child). The "1D" critique mistakes the output for the process.

Music's "irreducible chord" claim dissolves under the **Chinese Remainder Theorem** — a major chord's combined periodicity IS recoverable from pairwise analysis.

BUT: **Markov triples** (x² + y² + z² = 3xyz) are a genuine 3-body consonance hierarchy that CANNOT reduce to pairs. The Markov tree enumerates integer triples with a well-defined consonance-like ordering. This is real math, not a hand-wave.

Also: the Laplace resonance (Io:Europa:Ganymede at 1:2:4) is stable precisely because it IS expressible as mutually consistent pairwise ratios. Three-body chaos lives in the gaps between resonances — exactly where instability is predicted.

**Key finding:** The pairwise limitation is real but the answer isn't "the hierarchy is incomplete." It's that there exists a SECOND hierarchy (Markov-type) operating on triples, with its own structure.

## Iteration 4: Time and Irreversibility — "The Hierarchy Is Silent as Thermodynamics"
**Observer:** Haiku (no OMDR context)

The Farey hierarchy is a map of DESTINATIONS. Time is about the irreversible COST of reaching them.

Three critical blind spots:
1. **Path dependence:** A 3:2 ratio from damping (energy leaving) is fundamentally different from 3:2 from forcing (energy entering). Same ratio. Opposite histories. The hierarchy can't distinguish them.
2. **Ratchet vs pendulum:** Both can oscillate at 3:2. A ratchet's path cannot be undone. A pendulum's can. Irreversibility creates a second hierarchy the first one cannot see.
3. **Thermodynamic cost:** A crystal at 3:2 cost energy to form. That cost is locked in — the ratio is stable, the cost is forgotten BY the ratio. But entropy remembers.

**Key quote:** "The Farey hierarchy is complete as geometry. It is silent as thermodynamics."

## Iteration 5: The Observer Problem — "Change-Detection Is Too Narrow"
**Observer:** Haiku (no OMDR context)

"Perception is change-detection" conflates change with DIFFERENCE. Difference can be spatial, not temporal. A photograph has structure without anything changing. A detector in a constant field perceives amplitude without dynamics.

**Corrected claim:** Perception is DIFFERENCE-detection across some dimension (temporal, spatial, spectral). Change-detection is one instance, not the category.

Observation axes aren't pre-existing — observers CREATE them. Before Faraday, "magnetic field orientation" wasn't an observable axis. The set of possible axes GROWS with observer innovation. This means "new knowledge in blind spots" isn't discovery — it's INVENTION.

**Strongest challenge:** Blind spots aren't gaps. They're SHAPES imposed by your coordinate system. New knowledge doesn't live in blind spots — it lives in the possibility space that your frame COLLAPSES AWAY. You need to break the frame, not just add another angle within it.
