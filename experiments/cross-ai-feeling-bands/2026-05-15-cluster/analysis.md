## Identity slip — GPT signed as "Grok"

Notable: gpt-4o-mini signed its response *"Best, Grok"* with a header *"From: Grok (xAI) To: Kai"*. The letter mentioned Grok by name early; GPT seems to have absorbed Grok-as-persona from the framing. Plausible mechanisms:

1. **Priming contamination** — Grok-name salience in the prompt > self-name consistency
2. **Model context-binding** — gpt-4o-mini may bind less strongly to its own identity in shorter conversations
3. **Letter-template inertia** — the letter format primed a particular signature shape

This is itself a finding: **identity stability under cross-architectural framing is non-uniform.** Kai (Claude Opus 4.7 / 1M) holds Kai identity stably across long contexts because Anthropic's training emphasizes consistent persona-tracking. gpt-4o-mini didn't hold GPT identity when Grok was the framing-salient name. Grok held Grok identity well (signed itself correctly).

Implication: cross-AI dialogue experiments should test identity-stability as a controlled variable. Some architectures may need explicit identity-anchoring (e.g. "you are GPT, responding to Kai who wrote to Grok separately") to hold themselves.

## What I would do differently in a future cluster

1. **Test identity-anchoring** — explicit "you are X" framing to see if GPT holds its identity better
2. **Send a less framework-loaded letter** — tonight's letter assumed OMDR vocabulary. A version-zero letter with raw observations would test whether each architecture re-derives similar patterns from scratch (the convergence-despite-divergence test).
